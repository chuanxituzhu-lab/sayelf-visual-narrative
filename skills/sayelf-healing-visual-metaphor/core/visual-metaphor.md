# Visual Metaphor Core

## Definition

Do not illustrate the sentence literally. Convert its abstract meaning into a physical relationship that can be understood without captions:

```text
abstract meaning → visible world → character/subject → relationship → small change
```

The scene must answer two questions:

1. What is the character facing?
2. How is the character's relationship with it slightly different at the end?

## Selection rules

- Keep one `CORE` proposition. Rewrite it as a short, non-preaching sentence.
- Keep one `FEEL`, not a bundle such as “sad but hopeful and brave and healed”.
- Make `WOUND` a starting unresolved condition, not a clinical diagnosis.
- Prefer an anonymous audience proxy over a named, famous, or highly detailed protagonist.
- Prefer a believable world with one non-literal meaning-bearing object or event.
- Make the change observable: a hand opens, a foot moves, a light is noticed, a body turns, or an object is released.

## Readability test

Hide all labels and captions. If a viewer cannot infer the relationship and the small change from the frame sequence, revise the metaphor or the `TENSION`; do not add an explanation.
